# Brygidki Prison

## Overview
A prison in the city of [[Lemberg]], in the western part of Galicia, in the Carpathian region. The site of mysterious prisoner deaths that prompted Warden [[Josef Strasser]] to contact the Society.

## History (from Abraham's library research, 9/1/26)
- **Oldest prison in [[Lemberg]]** (Lviv) — the longest-standing detention facility in the region
- Originally built in **1614** as a monastery for the **Bridgetine Order** (Order of the Most Holy Savior), founded by Bridget of Sweden — so the connection to Scandinavia runs deep
- At its height, the convent housed around **30 nuns** from noble families
- In **1782**, the Austrian administration secularized the convent and converted it into a prison
- The name **"Brygidki"** literally means *Bridgetine nuns* in Polish — the building never shed its original name
- Under Austrian rule it became a **political and detention prison**: dissenters, protesters, and those who resisted Austrian authority — Polish, Jewish, and Ukrainian prisoners among them
- The prison holds a dark place in the history of multiple peoples

## Current Situation
- Managed by Warden [[Josef Strasser]]
- Several prisoners have fallen ill; some have died gruesome deaths
- The warden believes the deaths are supernatural in origin
- The prison has strict rules about what can be brought inside (no weapons, sharp objects, tobacco, alcohol, money, medicine, or fire-starting materials)

## Prohibited Items
Per the warden's letter, the following are not permitted inside:
- Explosives or weapons of any kind
- Anything sharp (knives, scissors)
- Tobacco or tobacco products
- Alcohol or alcohol-containing food/drinks
- Money or valuables
- Medicine or drugs
- Lighters or matches
